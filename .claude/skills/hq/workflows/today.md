# Today

## Purpose

Give the owner the three things that matter most today, each with one line on why, so they can start work without deciding from scratch.

## Use when

The owner asks "what should I work on today?", "what's my plan?", "what's most important right now?", or opens a session with no clear job in mind.

## Load first

- `business/goals.md` (this quarter's goals and the numbers we watch)
- The last three to five files in `memory/log/`
- `memory/decisions.md` (recent decisions that change priorities)
- `business/tools.md` (is a calendar or inbox connected?)
- `departments/sales/pipeline.md` (if it exists)
- Client notes in `departments/delivery/` and recent files in `outputs/delivery/` (if they exist)
- `departments/hq/context.md` (if it exists)

## Steps

1. **See what's already fixed.** If a calendar is connected in `tools.md`, read today's events. Note the meetings and how much free time is left. If no calendar is connected, ask one question: "Anything booked today I should plan around?" If `tools.md` says the calendar is connected but you can't read it right now, say so in one line and ask that same question instead.
2. **See what's waiting.** If an inbox is connected, scan unread messages from the last two days for anything from a lead, a client, or anything with a deadline. Don't open or summarise everything; just note what needs a reply today. Don't reply to anything. If `tools.md` says the inbox is connected but you can't read it right now, say so in one line and ask: "Anything in your inbox that needs a reply today?"
3. **Collect the candidates.** Gather possible jobs from:
   - the logs (anything marked "waiting on the owner" or left unfinished)
   - the sales pipeline (leads waiting on a follow-up, proposals due, calls to prep for)
   - client work (sessions today, promised deliverables, follow-ups owed)
   - goals (the one action that would move the goal furthest behind). If a goal deadline, the quarter, an offer start date or a launch date in the business files is already in the past (check today's date), say so in one line and ask whether it still stands, or suggest running [quarterly-goals](quarterly-goals.md), before planning around it.
4. **Pick three.** Rank by: money or a client waiting on it first, then anything with a deadline today or tomorrow, then the goal furthest behind. Choose three at most. If there are clearly fewer than three things that matter, give fewer. Each item must be a concrete action ("send the proposal to the lead from Tuesday's call"), not an area ("sales").
5. **Say why.** One line each, tied to something real: a goal, a date, a person waiting. If you can't point to a reason, it isn't a top three.
6. **Name what can wait.** One short line listing what you deliberately left off, so the owner knows it was seen.
7. **Offer the first step.** End by offering to start on number one ("Want me to draft that follow-up now?"), using the right department's workflow.

## Check before handing over

- Exactly three items or fewer, each a specific action someone could start in the next ten minutes.
- Every "why" points to a real goal, date, client or lead in the files or connected tools. No invented deadlines or numbers.
- Nothing scheduled clashes with what the calendar shows.
- Client details stay in the chat and this folder; nothing is sent anywhere.

## Save to

Show the plan in the chat. Only save it if the owner asks: `outputs/hq/YYYY-MM-DD-today.md`. At the end of the session, note in `memory/log/YYYY-MM-DD.md` which of the three got done.

## Needs a yes

Nothing in this workflow sends or publishes. If the owner asks you to act on an item (reply to an email, move a meeting, invite someone), that action follows its own workflow and needs a yes first, showing exactly what will be sent and to whom.
