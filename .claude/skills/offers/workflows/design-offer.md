# Design an offer

## Purpose

Shape a clear offer from the customer's problem: the result they get, how it's delivered, what's included, two or three price options and a plain name. Save it to `business/offers.md` once the owner is happy with the wording.

## Use when

The owner says "help me create a new offer", "I want to package what I do", "turn my one-off sessions into a programme", "my offer isn't selling, can we rework it?", or wants a smaller or bigger version of something they already sell.

## Load first

- `business/customers.md` (struggles, wants, words they use, why they hesitate)
- `business/offers.md` (what's already for sale, so the new one fits alongside)
- `business/profile.md` (what makes the business different)
- `business/goals.md` (what this offer needs to achieve)
- `business/voice.md` (for the name and wording)
- `departments/offers/context.md` (if it exists)

## Steps

1. **The customer's problem.** Ask who this is for and what they're stuck on, unless `customers.md` already makes it clear. Write the problem in the customer's own words, one or two sentences. If you have no real customer words, ask the owner for something a customer actually said.
2. **The outcome.** What's different for them afterwards? Make it specific and believable ("has a booked-out diary for March", "has a finished website they can edit themselves"), not a vague promise. Ask the owner what results past customers have actually had. Don't promise anything they can't deliver.
3. **The format.** Agree how it's delivered: one-to-one or group, live or done-for-you, in person or online, how long it runs, how often they meet. Ask one question at a time. Check the owner's time: roughly how many hours each customer takes.
4. **What's included.** List each part and what it does for the customer ("weekly 45-minute call: so you never get stuck for more than a week"). Cut anything that's only there to make the list longer.
5. **Price options.** Offer two or three ways to pay or buy (for example: pay in full, pay monthly, a lighter version). Don't pick numbers yourself. Ask the owner for the price they have in mind, or run [price-check](price-check.md) if they're unsure.
6. **The name.** Suggest three names: one plain and descriptive ("Six-Week Website Sprint"), one built on the outcome, one built on the customer's words. Avoid anything from the "never use" list in `voice.md`. Let the owner pick or change it.
7. **Objections.** From "Why they hesitate to buy" in `customers.md`, list the top three objections and a short honest answer to each, built from what's actually included.
8. **Show the full offer** in the same shape as `business/offers.md` (who it's for, the result, what's included, format and length, price and payment options, how to buy, main objections and answers, status). Ask: "Happy with this wording? I'll add it to your offers file."

## Check before handing over

- A stranger could read it and say who it's for, what they get and what it costs.
- The outcome is something the owner has said they can deliver. No invented results, numbers or guarantees.
- Every price came from the owner (or from a price-check they agreed to).
- The owner's time per customer fits the price and their available hours.
- The name and wording sound like `voice.md`, not like a generic course launch.

## Save to

Draft: `outputs/offers/YYYY-MM-DD-offer-<short-name>.md`. After a yes on the wording, add a new `## Offer: <name>` section to `business/offers.md` with status `coming soon` (or update the existing section in place if this reworks a current offer). Record the new offer in `memory/decisions.md` with today's date.

## Needs a yes

Writing to `business/offers.md`: show the exact section first. Putting the offer or its price into any live tool (a booking page, a checkout, a payment link) or announcing it anywhere needs its own explicit yes.
