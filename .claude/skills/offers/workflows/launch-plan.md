# Launch plan

## Purpose

Plan the launch of one offer from start to finish: a dated timeline, the content to make, the emails to send and how sales follows up, so the owner knows exactly what happens each day.

## Use when

The owner says "help me launch my new programme", "plan a launch for…", "I want to open doors again in March", "run a promotion for my package", or has just finished [design-offer](design-offer.md).

## Load first

- The offer in `business/offers.md` (it must have a name, what's included, a price and how to buy)
- `business/customers.md` (words, struggles, objections)
- `business/goals.md` (how many sales this launch needs to make)
- `business/tools.md` (email tool, booking tool, payment tool, social accounts)
- `departments/marketing/context.md` and `departments/sales/context.md` (if they exist)
- `departments/offers/context.md` (if it exists)

## Steps

1. **Pin the brief.** You need: the offer, the launch date, the date sales close (if they do), and the target number of sales. Ask one question at a time for anything missing. If the offer has no price or no way to buy yet, stop and offer [design-offer](design-offer.md) first.
2. **Pick the shape.** Suggest one of three, sized to the owner's time and list:
   - **Quiet launch:** personal messages to past customers and warm leads, plus a few posts. One to two weeks.
   - **Standard launch:** a warm-up week of content, an open week with emails and posts, a close.
   - **Event launch:** a free workshop, call or challenge that leads into the offer.
   Say which you'd pick and why in one line. Let them choose.
3. **Timeline.** Lay out every day from warm-up to close in a table: date, what happens, which channel, who does it (the owner or the brain), and status. Include the real dates.
4. **Content.** List the posts needed (topic and angle for each, tied to a customer struggle or objection). Don't write them here; mark each one to be made with the **marketing** skill's `social-post` or `content-week` workflow.
5. **Emails.** List each email with its send date, subject idea and one-line purpose (announce, story, objections, last chance). Mark each one to be written with the **marketing** skill's `newsletter` workflow.
6. **Sales follow-up.** Plan how interested people get a reply: who gets a personal message, when, and what triggers a follow-up (clicked but didn't buy, asked a question, booked a call). Mark these for the **sales** skill's `lead-follow-up` workflow.
7. **Before launch checklist.** Payment or booking link works, sales page is ready (offer [sales-page](sales-page.md)), delivery is ready for new customers, calendar has space for calls.
8. **Afterwards.** Add a review date a week after close: sales against target, what worked, what to change. That review can run as an HQ weekly review.

## Check before handing over

- Every date is a real calendar date and the timeline fits the owner's available hours.
- The sales target and the price are the owner's, not invented. The plan shows how many buyers it needs.
- Every piece of content and every email links back to a real customer struggle or objection.
- No fake urgency: deadlines and limited places are only used if they're true.
- Nothing is scheduled or sent by this plan.

## Save to

`outputs/offers/YYYY-MM-DD-launch-plan-<offer>.md`, with the timeline table, the content list, the email list, the follow-up plan and the checklist. Show the timeline in the chat.

## Needs a yes

Nothing here posts or sends. Each post, email, message or calendar invite made from the plan needs its own yes before it goes out, showing the final text, who it goes to, where and when. Setting up or changing a price or payment link is a money action and needs an explicit yes.
