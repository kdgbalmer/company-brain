# LinkedIn

Rules for a small service business, usually posting from the owner's personal profile (which normally reaches far more people than a company page). Platforms change often: anything marked "check current limits" should be checked in the app before relying on it.

## Formats

- **Text post**: the core format. A clear opinion, lesson or story from the owner's work.
- **Text with one image**: a photo of the owner, a simple graphic, or a screenshot that backs up the point.
- **Document (PDF) carousel**: swipeable slides uploaded as a PDF. Good for frameworks, checklists and step-by-steps.
- **Short video**: the owner talking to camera. Works well vertical or square.
- **Poll**: for a quick question that starts a conversation. Use rarely.

## Sizes

| Format | Size | Shape |
|---|---|---|
| Image (best on mobile) | 1080 × 1350 px | 4:5 portrait |
| Square image | 1080 × 1080 px | 1:1 |
| Landscape image | 1200 × 627 px | about 1.91:1 |
| Document carousel slides | 1080 × 1350 px or 1080 × 1080 px | Keep every slide the same size |
| Video | 1080 × 1920 px or 1080 × 1350 px | 9:16 or 4:5 |

## Text

- **The first two lines are everything.** Only those show before "...more" (fewer on a phone). Make line one a short, specific hook: a clear opinion, a surprising number from the business, or a moment from a client story. Line two should make them click "more".
- **Length**: 100 to 300 words works well for most posts. The maximum is around 3,000 characters (check current limits).
- **Line breaks**: one or two sentences per paragraph, with a blank line between. No walls of text.
- **Tone**: professional but human. First person, real experience, a clear point of view. No corporate speak.
- **Links**: posts with links in them may be shown to fewer people. Many people put the link in the first comment and say "link in the comments". Tell the owner this is a common habit, not a rule.
- **One next step**: a question to answer in the comments, "message me", or "link in the comments".

## Hashtags

0 to 3, at the end. They matter little on LinkedIn now. Only use specific ones that describe the topic.

## Caption vs on-screen text

For video, add captions burned into the video (many people watch with the sound off) and a short hook in the first seconds. The post text carries the context and the next step. For document carousels, slide 1 is the hook; the post text should give a reason to swipe.

## What tends to work for service businesses

- Lessons from real client work, told as short stories (no client names without permission).
- A clear opinion on a common mistake in the owner's field.
- Behind the scenes on how the owner works, prices or decides.
- Frameworks and checklists as document carousels.
- Replying to every comment in the first hour or so after posting, and commenting thoughtfully on posts by people the ideal customer follows.

## How posting works

- **Connected tool**: if `business/tools.md` shows LinkedIn or a scheduling tool as connected, the brain can schedule the post after a yes.
- **Manual**: the brain gives the text and image or PDF. The owner opens LinkedIn, clicks "Start a post", pastes the text, adds the image or document and posts. LinkedIn also lets you schedule from the post box (the clock icon).
