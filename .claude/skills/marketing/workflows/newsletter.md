# Newsletter

## Purpose

Write one email to the business's list, about one idea, in the owner's voice, with subject line options, preview text and one clear call to action.

## Use when

The owner asks for an email to their list: "write this week's newsletter", "email my list about the new workshop", "I want to send something to my subscribers about pricing mistakes".

## Load first

- `business/voice.md` (tone rules, samples, corrections)
- `business/customers.md` (their struggles, words and objections)
- The offer the email points to, from `business/offers.md` (if any)
- `business/goals.md` (if the owner hasn't given an idea)
- `business/tools.md` (which email tool they use and whether it's connected)
- `departments/marketing/context.md`
- The last two or three newsletters in `outputs/marketing/` (to keep the format consistent and avoid repeats)

## Steps

1. **Pin the brief.** You need: the one idea, who it goes to (the whole list or a group), and the one thing the reader should do (reply, click, book, buy). If the owner has no idea yet, offer three, each built on a struggle or question from `customers.md`, and let them pick. If anything else is missing, ask one question. Otherwise state your assumption in one line and carry on.
2. **Subject lines.** Write five, each a different type: curiosity, a plain promise, a question in the customer's words, a number or list, a line from the story. Keep them short enough to read in full on a phone. No all-caps, no fake "Re:" or "Fwd:".
3. **Preview text.** One line that adds to the subject instead of repeating it. This is the grey text people see next to the subject in their inbox.
4. **Write the body.**
   - Open with a first line that pulls the reader into the idea straight away. No "Hope you're well", no "In this email".
   - One idea only, carried by a story, example or real moment from the business.
   - Short paragraphs, one to three sentences. Plain formatting: it should read like a person wrote it to one person.
   - Aim for 200 to 500 words, unless the owner's past newsletters are clearly longer or shorter.
   - Sign off the way the owner signs off in their samples.
5. **One call to action.** One link or one action, said plainly, and repeated at most once (for example in a P.S.). If it points to an offer, use the real price and booking link from `offers.md`. If the link isn't there, put `[link]` and ask for it.
6. **Put it in the email tool (optional).** If `tools.md` shows the email tool as connected, offer to create the email there as an unsent draft so the owner can preview it. Do it when they say so. If it isn't connected, give them the text ready to copy and paste.

## Check before handing over

- Read it as the ideal customer opening it on their phone: would the subject make them open it, and would they know what to do at the end?
- Is there exactly one idea and one call to action?
- Could this email have come from any other business? If yes, add a specific detail from the business files.
- No words from the "never use" list in `voice.md`. No invented results, testimonials or numbers.
- Every link and price is real, or clearly marked `[link]` / `[price]` for the owner to fill in.

## Save to

`outputs/marketing/YYYY-MM-DD-newsletter-<short-topic>.md`, containing the subject line options (recommended one first), the preview text, the body and the call to action. Show the recommended subject, preview text and body in the chat as well.

## Needs a yes

Sending or scheduling the email to any list or group, and sending a test email to anyone. Show the final subject, preview text and body, which list or group it goes to (with the number of people if the tool shows it), and the send time first. If the owner has allowed this without asking in `business/rules.md`, send and confirm.
