# Lead magnet

## Purpose

Create a free resource the ideal customer will gladly swap their email address for, that solves one small part of their problem and leads naturally to a paid offer. Includes the content itself, the landing page copy and the email that delivers it.

## Use when

The owner wants something to grow their list or warm up leads: "make me a freebie", "I need a lead magnet for my coaching programme", "write a checklist people can download", "something to give away in exchange for an email".

## Load first

- `business/customers.md` (struggles, words, where they spend time, why they hesitate)
- The offer it leads to, from `business/offers.md`
- `business/voice.md`
- `business/brand.md` (for the cover and layout)
- `business/goals.md` (if list size or enquiries is a goal)
- `business/tools.md` (email tool, landing page tool, Canva)
- `departments/marketing/context.md`

## Steps

1. **Pin the brief.** You need: the offer it leads to, and who exactly it's for. If the business has more than one offer and the owner hasn't said, ask one question: "Which offer should this lead people towards?"
2. **Pick the format.** Choose the one that fits how the ideal customer works and what the offer solves:
   - **Checklist**: for a process they keep getting wrong. Quick to use.
   - **Short guide** (5 to 10 pages): for a problem that needs some explaining.
   - **Template or swipe file**: for something they'd otherwise write from scratch.
   - **Self-check quiz or scorecard**: for people who don't yet know what their problem is.
   - **Mini email course** (3 to 5 emails): for a change that takes a few days.
   - **Resource list**: for people who need the right tools or suppliers.

   It should take under 15 minutes to use and give a real win, while making it clear the full fix is the paid offer. Suggest three titles, recommend one format in one line with the reason, and ask: "Go with this one?"
3. **Outline.** Show the sections or items in order, each with one line on what it covers. Ask one question: "Anything to add or cut?" If the owner already said "just make it", skip the question.
4. **Write the full content.** In the owner's voice, using the customer's own words for problems. Use the business's real methods, steps and examples. Where a real example or result would help but isn't in the files, put `[example from your work]` for the owner to fill in. The last page or email bridges to the offer: what it is, who it's for, and how to book or buy, with the real link from `offers.md`.
5. **Landing page copy.** Headline (the result, in the customer's words), one supporting line, three to five short bullets on what they get, the button text, and one line on what happens to their email address. Keep it to one goal: get the email.
6. **Delivery email.** Subject line, a short body that gives them the link to the resource, tells them how to get the most out of it in one or two lines, says what emails they'll get next, and ends with one next step (reply with a question, or the offer if it fits).
7. **Layout (optional).** For the cover image, run [branded-graphic](branded-graphic.md). For the full document: if `tools.md` shows Canva connected, offer to lay it out there; otherwise give it as clean text the owner can paste into a document and save as a PDF.

## Check before handing over

- Would the ideal customer actually want this enough to give their email? Is the title specific, not "Ultimate guide to success"?
- Does it give a real, quick win on its own, and lead clearly to the offer?
- No invented results, testimonials, client stories or statistics. Gaps are marked for the owner.
- Landing page and delivery email each have exactly one call to action.
- Every link and price is real, or clearly marked for the owner to fill in.

## Save to

`outputs/marketing/YYYY-MM-DD-lead-magnet-<short-name>.md`, containing the format and title, the outline, the full content, the landing page copy and the delivery email. Any graphics go next to it as `.html` files. Show the title, outline and landing page headline in the chat, and say where the rest is.

## Needs a yes

Publishing the landing page, switching on the delivery email or any automation in a connected tool, and sending it to anyone. Show exactly what will go live, where, and who will get which email first. Creating unsent drafts in a connected tool is fine once the owner asks for it.
